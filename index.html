Translate-and-Revise: Boosting Large Language Models for Constrained Translation

LLMs skip required terms when they are confident in a more common wording. Translate-and-Revise (TAR) tells the model exactly which constraints it missed and lets it revise.

Pengcheng Huang1,* Yongyu Mu1,* Yuzhang Wu1 Bei Li1 Chunyang Xiao3 Tong Xiao1,2,† Jingbo Zhu1,2
1NLP Lab, School of Computer Science and Engineering, Northeastern University, China 2NiuTrans Research 3JP Morgan
*Equal contribution  ·  †Corresponding author
arXiv:2407.13164 ACL Anthology PDF BibTeX

TL;DRTAR is a training-free prompting method for constrained machine translation. An LLM first translates with the constraints in its prompt; a rule-based check lists the constraints the translation missed; the LLM then revises its own translation given exactly those constraints, and the loop repeats until every constraint is met or a round limit is reached. Across four constrained translation tasks it improves constraint-based translation accuracy by about 15% over standard LLM translation.

Abstract

Imposing constraints on machine translation systems presents a challenging issue because these systems are not trained to make use of constraints in generating adequate, fluent translations. In this paper, we leverage the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) for constrained translation, given that LLMs can easily adapt to this task by taking translation instructions and constraints as prompts. However, LLMs cannot always guarantee the adequacy of translation, and, in some cases, ignore the given constraints. This is in part because LLMs might be overly confident in their predictions, overriding the influence of the constraints. To overcome this overriding behaviour, we propose to add a revision process that encourages LLMs to correct the outputs by prompting them about the constraints that have not yet been met. We evaluate our approach on four constrained translation tasks, encompassing both lexical and structural constraints in multiple constraint domains. Experiments show 15% improvement in constraint-based translation accuracy over standard LLMs and the approach also significantly outperforms neural machine translation (NMT) state-of-the-art methods.

Why LLMs ignore constraints

Putting constraints in the prompt helps, but it does not guarantee they are followed. Translating an English sentence into Chinese with the constraint COVID-19 → 新型冠状病毒, an open-source LLM still produced the shorter, more common 新冠 more than 80% of the time. Of 20 randomly sampled errors on WMT21 Terminology Translation, 19 were close in meaning to the required term and generated with high confidence, and that confidence barely changed whether or not the constraint was in the prompt. The model is overconfident in the mainstream translation, the pattern McKenzie et al. call the memo trap.

Method: translate, then revise

Stage 1

Translate with a constraint-aware prompt

Given source sentence $X$ and bilingual constraints $\langle S,T\rangle=\{\langle s_1,t_1\rangle,\dots,\langle s_k,t_k\rangle\}$, the LLM must produce $Y=\mathrm{Trans}(f(X,\langle S,T\rangle))$ with every target term $t_i$ in $Y$. A natural-language instruction turns the LLM into a constraint-aware translator:

Translate prompt

Translate the sentence from [src-lang] to [tgt-lang], ensuring the provided constraints are reflected in the translation.
The constraints are given in no specific order. Only provide the translation result.
Sentence: X
Constraints: ⟨S,T⟩
Output:
Stage 2

Revise with the constraints that were missed

A rule-based checker (the same procedure used to compute CCR) finds the uncompleted constraints $\langle S,T\rangle^{un}_i$. The LLM, now acting as a reviser, receives the source, its current translation, all constraints and the uncompleted ones. The loop stops when every constraint is met or a maximum number of rounds is reached:

$$Y_{i+1}=\mathrm{Revise}\big(f(X,\ \langle S,T\rangle,\ \langle S,T\rangle^{un}_{i},\ Y_{i})\big)$$

Revise prompt

Given a sentence in [src-lang], its constraints, and its current translation in [tgt-lang]:
Original [src-lang] sentence: X
Constraints: ⟨S,T⟩
Current translation: Y_flawed
Please provide a revised translation based on the following error message, ensuring that all the constraints are accurately reflected in the translation:
Uncompleted constraints: ⟨S,T⟩_un
Revised translation result:

Analysis

  • 1
    TAR also fixes NMT output. Revising the translations of a WMT21-winning, constraint-agnostic NMT model raises BLEU by 0.7 and 0.8 and CCR by 10.7% and 14.3% on WMT21 TT and ETC, with no forced decoding or extra training.
  • 2
    It works across LLMs. On WMT21 terminology (En–Zh, En–Ru), TAR raises average CCR by 20% for GPT-3, 11.7% for GPT-4 and 14.4% for Qwen-14B-Chat over a conventional translation prompt.
  • 3
    The first revision matters most. With gpt-3.5 on WMT21 TT En→Zh, CCR goes 92.6 → 95.1 → 95.6 → 95.9 over three rounds. Using a different revision template in each round beats repeating one template.
  • 4
    Precise feedback is essential. Removing the original constraints from the reviser's input hurts CCR most. Letting an LLM decide which constraints are unmet can make results worse, because it often wrongly believes a constraint is satisfied.

FAQ

What is Translate-and-Revise (TAR)?

TAR is a prompting method for constrained machine translation with large language models, from the CCL 2024 paper by Pengcheng Huang, Yongyu Mu and colleagues at Northeastern University (China). The LLM translates with the constraints in its prompt, then revises its own output after being told which constraints it missed, repeating until all constraints are satisfied.

Why do LLMs ignore translation constraints?

They are overconfident in the translation they saw most often during pre-training. For example, an LLM asked to render "COVID-19" as 新型冠状病毒 kept producing the more common 新冠. The paper links this to the "memo trap" and finds that the model's confidence in such tokens hardly changes when the constraint is added to the prompt.

Does TAR need training or fine-tuning?

No. TAR only changes the prompts and adds a revision loop. The paper applies it to gpt-3.5-turbo, GPT-3 (text-davinci-003), GPT-4 and Qwen-14B-Chat, and also uses an LLM reviser to correct the output of a conventional NMT system.

How does TAR know which constraints are unmet?

With a rule-based check, the same procedure used to compute the constraint completion rate. The paper also tried letting the LLM detect unmet constraints itself; that feedback was less accurate and could degrade the translation.

How does TAR compare with dedicated constrained-translation systems?

Data-augmentation NMT methods are nearly perfect on IATE, Wiktionary and LXM, whose constraints are often seen in training, but reach only 50–73% CCR on the diverse ETC entity test set, where TAR reaches 89.8–97.6%. On WMT21 terminology, TAR exceeds the top shared-task submissions in CCR (95.9 vs. 88.6 En→Zh; 97.5 vs. 90.9 En→Ru), though HW-TSC has higher BLEU on En→Zh.

How many revision rounds are needed?

Most of the gain comes from the first revision; later rounds add progressively less. Varying the revision template across rounds, similar to a prompt ensemble, works better than reusing one template.

中文简介

Translate-and-Revise（TAR）：用“翻译—修订”提升大语言模型的约束翻译能力（CCL 2024，第二十三届中国计算语言学大会），作者为东北大学自然语言处理实验室的黄鹏程（Pengcheng Huang）与 Yongyu Mu（共同一作）等，通讯作者为肖桐。

约束翻译要求译文必须使用指定的术语、实体译名或保留 XML 标签结构。把约束写进提示词后，大模型仍会因为对常见译法过度自信而忽略约束，例如要求把 “COVID-19” 译为“新型冠状病毒”，开源模型八成以上的情况仍译成“新冠”。

TAR 无需训练：先用带约束的提示让大模型翻译，再用规则检查哪些约束没有满足，把原文、当前译文、全部约束和未满足的约束一起交给大模型修订，循环直到约束全部满足或达到轮数上限。论文在通用词汇约束（IATE、Wiktionary）、术语翻译（WMT21）、实体翻译（ETC）和结构化文档翻译（LXM）四类任务上评测，约束完成率比普通大模型翻译平均提升约 15%，在 WMT21 术语翻译任务上的约束完成率超过当年的最佳参赛系统；TAR 还可以用来修订传统神经机器翻译系统的输出。
